# Relay Everything app

Relay Everything keeps your thoughts. Type a note on your phone and it stays on your phone: you need no account, no key and no signal, and the app sends nothing. When you want to do more with your notes, sign in: each note then goes to your own Relay Everything server, which can pass it to your own database and your AI. Once you sign in, the app can also send your steps, heart rate, sleep, workouts and a history of the places you went. Your AI reads your health and places; this app only sends them.

This is the phone app, for iPhone and Android. It is written in Flutter. The same app, built for the browser, is the Relay Everything page at the top address of each server, such as `https://app.relayeverything.com/`: [The web build](#the-web-build).

## What it does

- **Home.** The app opens on *Home*, a screen of folders like Apple Notes' *Folders*. The first card holds *Notes* (every note), *Voice notes* and *Shares* (notes with a photo, a file or a link), each with its count. *On this phone* holds *Location*, *Health*, *Calendar* and *Photos*, each with its status, such as *Off* or *Save battery · Sent 14:05*. Home has no setup: your account is in Settings. Tap a header to fold it; the phone remembers. *Search* and *New note* stay at the bottom.
- **Notes.** A notes folder lists its notes, newest first, under its name, with *‹ Home* at the top left, in groups: *Today*, *Yesterday*, *Previous 7 days*, *Previous 30 days*, then by month. Each row shows the first line in bold, the time, the rest of the words, a *Voice* or *Typed* tag, and a small picture if the note has one. Under the title, a line gives the count and the sync state, such as *5 notes · Synced*, with a dot that pulses only while *Syncing*. With sending off, it says *On this phone*. With sending on, each note shows *Sent* or *Waiting to send*, and a waiting note goes on the next sync. With sending on, *Sync now*, a small ↻ button, follows the sync state: tap it, or pull the list down, to sync. Tap a row to open the whole note: its words, pictures, files, and *Play* for a voice note. Tap the words to change them, as in Apple Notes.
- **Search.** Type in *Search* at the bottom, and the list shows only the notes with those words in their words, their place or their file names. On Home it finds notes in every folder; in a folder, only that folder's notes. It searches this phone only, with no signal. *Cancel* shows all the notes again.
- **Settings and your page.** The round gear button at the top right of Home opens *Settings*. While the phone sends to a Relay Everything server, the *Account* page holds *Open your Relay Everything page*. It opens `<your-relay-server>/` in the phone's browser, where you sign in: this same app, built for the browser.
- **New note.** Tap the round green *New note* button at the bottom, type, and tap *Save*. The first line shows in bold. With *Pin notes to where I am* on, the note keeps where you are. This works with no signal. With sending off, the phone does not look up the place's name, so the note shows *Pinned*.
- **Voice notes.** Hold *New note*: an amber circle fills round it in half a second, the phone buzzes, and a full screen opens with a clock and amber bars that follow your voice. Until the first voice note, a *Hold to talk* tip shows above the button. Let go: it keeps listening. Talk, then tap anywhere on the screen, or use the back gesture, to save, or tap the *X* at the top right to keep nothing. The bars use the sound level only to draw the screen. The phone writes the words itself with whisper.cpp, with no signal and no server, and the recording stays on the phone, out of iCloud and Google backups. The first time, the app asks before it gets the 57 MB speech model from whisper.cpp's page on Hugging Face; *Remove* in *Settings* deletes it. A tap shorter than half a second opens *New note* and records nothing. A recording with no words keeps nothing. With sending on, only the words go to your server, as a typed note.
- **Photos and files.** In *New note*, tap *Add photo or file* to add up to 10 photos or files, each up to 25 MB. They stay on the phone and show on the note's row and its page; tap one to open it. With sending on, each file goes after its note.
- **Apple Shortcuts** (iPhone, iOS 16 and later). Three actions: *Add a note*, *Get notes* and *Start a voice note*. *Start a voice note* opens the same full-screen recording: tap anywhere to save, or the *X* to keep nothing. A note from a shortcut is kept and sent as a typed note is. *Get notes* reads only this phone.
- **Apple Watch** (watchOS 10 and later). The Relay Everything watch app comes with the iPhone app. Tap the amber *Talk* button, talk, then tap anywhere to save, or tap the *X* to keep nothing. A watch face button or the *Start a voice note* shortcut, such as on the Action button, starts a recording too. The note waits on the watch until your iPhone is near, then the iPhone writes the words and keeps it in *Voice notes*, as a voice note made on the phone. The watch shows how many notes wait. A watch note keeps no place, and the watch sends nothing to a server.
- **Share from any app.** Share a web page, text, photos or a file into Relay Everything, add words, and tap *Save*. On iPhone and Android, the share card has the brand's paper color, the Inter font and a green *Save*, in light and dark.
- **Settings.** Open it with the gear on Home. The Account row comes first, as the Apple Account row on an iPhone. Then three groups: *Appearance* (*System*, *Light* or *Dark*), *Notes* (*Pin notes to where I am*) and *Voice notes* (the speech model). The last line gives the app's version and build.
- **Sign in.** Signed out, the Account row says *Sign in to Relay Everything* and *Your notes stay on this phone until you sign in.* A tap opens *Sign in*: *Sign in to connect* first, then *Scan a key*, *Use another server* and *Send to my own webhook instead*. Signing in starts sending in the same step, to one place, never both. For your own webhook, you type its address, copy its secret, and need no account: [`lib/data/webhook.dart`](lib/data/webhook.dart) signs each message in the server's webhook format. If you have notes from before, the app asks if they go too: *Send them*, or *Only new notes*. Signed in, the row shows your email and your server; with your own webhook, *My own webhook* and its address.
- **The Account page.** A tap on the Account row, once you sign in, opens it: your email and server, *AI apps* and *Webhooks*, *Open your Relay Everything page*, *Server*, and *Sign out* in red. *Sign out* asks first, then removes this phone's key and stops sending. Your notes from this phone stay; notes from your other devices leave this phone. With your own webhook, the page shows its address, *Send a test*, *Sign in to Relay Everything* and *Stop sending*, which keeps the webhook's address and secret on the phone. *Sign in to Relay Everything* opens the sign-in with no webhook choice; once signed in, notes go to Relay Everything, health, places, calendar and photos stay on the webhook (*Only my own webhook*), the app asks whether to send the notes that went only to the webhook, and a message says to add the webhook on your Relay Everything page to get notes there too.
- **Health, places, calendar and photos only to your own webhook.** Signed in, the Account page has a group *Health, places, calendar and photos*: *Relay Everything* (the default) or *Only my own webhook*. With the webhook, the phone sends health samples, location points, calendar events and new photos straight to your receiver, and Relay Everything never gets them. Notes, their files and a note's pinned place still sync through Relay Everything. With no webhook kept, the pick opens the webhook's form first; going back changes nothing. Notes and private data each go in their own order, and a receiver that is down holds back no note. If the phone loses the webhook, such as after a restore, private data waits on the phone, never goes to Relay Everything, and the page shows *Set your webhook again*. What went to Relay Everything before stays on your page. *Sign out* keeps the choice and the webhook. The folders then say *Sent to your webhook*.
- **Health** (only with sending on). The app reads steps, heart rate, sleep and workouts from Apple Health or Health Connect from the moment you turn it on, and sends them about every hour, also while the app is closed. The *Health* folder shows when they last went. Signed out, it says Health works once you sign in, and *Sign in* opens the same sign-in as Settings.
- **Location history** (only with sending on), in the *Location* folder. On Android, the app keeps a point each 100 m you move. On an iPhone, the phone keeps points while the app is closed: *Save battery* at each move of about 500 m and each arrival, or *Every 100 m*.
- **Photos** (only with sending on), in the *Photos* folder. The app sends a copy of each new photo you take, at most 1,600 px on its long side, with when and where you took it, so your AI can see it. Photos wait for Wi-Fi unless *Send photos on mobile data* is on, and never hold back a note. They go where your health and places go: your Relay Everything server, or your own webhook as `photo.created` messages. To add a photo to one note, use *Add photo or file*.
- **AI apps and Webhooks.** On the Account page, while the phone sends to a Relay Everything server, these rows show the names of the AI apps and webhooks you connected on your page, such as *Claude*. A tap opens that part of your page in the browser, to add or remove one there.
- **Your server, your key.** The app talks only to the server you connect it to, with a key for this phone alone. The key adds data, reads and changes your notes and their files, and reads your account's email and the names of your AI apps and webhooks. It reads no health, places, calendar events, chats or photos. You can turn off one phone's key and keep the others.

Each permission is asked for only when you turn on its setting. Signed out, the app keeps no health or location history and makes no network call, except to get the speech model after you tap *Get it*.

## Get the app

- **Android:** download the `.apk` file from [the newest release](https://github.com/matthewwong525/RelayEverything/releases/latest) and open it on your phone. Android asks once to allow installs from your browser. Test builds from `main` are on [Releases](https://github.com/matthewwong525/RelayEverything/releases), marked *Pre-release*.
- **iPhone:** install the newest build from TestFlight, from the invite the owner sends you. After the first App Store release, the App Store has it too.

## Connect it to a server

Each phone sends with its own key. Sign in to your Relay Everything page and tap *Add a device*. The key shows once, as a QR code. In the app, open Settings, tap *Sign in to Relay Everything*, then tap *Scan a key*, or paste the key.

Or connect with no key to copy: tap *Sign in to connect* (*Use another server* for a server that is not `app.relayeverything.com`). The app opens the server's sign-in page inside the app, the person taps *Connect*, and the app trades the page's one-time code, with a secret only it holds, for its own key at `sign-in/phone/key`. The key holds the server's address. The app checks the key with the server's `key` route, which gives only the device name, never your data. The `connections` route gives only your account's email and the names of your AI apps and webhooks. To try a preview, open the Account page, tap *Server*, and enter the preview's address.

*Sign out*, on the Account page, removes the key and turns sending off. Your notes from this phone stay on the phone.

The key is kept in the iPhone Keychain or the Android Keystore, never in the app's own files.

## Build it yourself

1. Install Flutter (the version is in `pubspec.yaml`): https://docs.flutter.dev/get-started/install
2. Get the packages: `flutter pub get`
3. Check and test: `flutter analyze` and `flutter test`
4. Run it on a phone: `flutter run`

### The web build

The Relay Everything page at a server's `/` is this app, built for the browser from its own entry, [`lib/main_web.dart`](lib/main_web.dart). It signs in against the server it is served from, with that server's sign-in, and keeps no notes, key or health data in the browser. A wide window shows three panes like Notes on a Mac; under 700 px it shows the phone layout. What the page does is on the website: [On a computer](https://relayeverything.com/app/on-a-computer/).

- **Try it:** `flutter run -d chrome -t lib/main_web.dart`.
- **Build it:** `node scripts/build-relay-web.mjs` runs `flutter build web -t lib/main_web.dart --base-href /apps/relay/ --no-web-resources-cdn --release`, then copies `mobile/build/web/` to `app/public/apps/relay/`. The server opens the page at `/`, and loads its files, API and sign-in from `/apps/relay/`. `scripts/publish-relay-server.mjs` and the release zip run it; the company app's build does not.
- **No Google fonts:** the engine gets its fonts for emoji and other scripts from `fonts.gstatic.com` unless told otherwise. [`web/flutter_bootstrap.js`](web/flutter_bootstrap.js) points it at the page's own `fallback-fonts/`, and the build script reads the engine's font list from the built `main.dart.js` and fetches each file there, once, into `mobile/build/fallback-fonts/`. Under `flutter run`, emoji show as boxes, because that folder is missing.

### Builds on GitHub

Each push that changes `mobile/` runs [`.github/workflows/mobile-ci.yml`](../.github/workflows/mobile-ci.yml). Its first job, `plan`, runs [`scripts/app-release.mjs`](../scripts/app-release.mjs) `version` to get the run's version and build number: you never edit the version in `pubspec.yaml`. Then it analyzes and tests, and builds both apps:

- **A branch or pull request builds and sends nothing.** Both builds start at the same time as the tests, so the run ends in about 6 minutes; a failed test still makes the run fail. The `.apk` is kept with the run, for 64-bit ARM phones only, and the iPhone app is signed and checked, so a broken build shows before it lands.
- **A push to `main` sends a beta.** Its builds start only after the tests pass, and its `.apk` holds every kind of Android phone. TestFlight gets the iPhone build, and a *Pre-release* tagged `mobile-v<version>+<build>` gets the `.apk`. It is never the latest release. Only the newest change sends: when a newer commit on `main` changes `mobile/`, the older run sends nothing, as the newer run sends its own build. A beta's version is the newest release's with its last number one higher, such as `1.1.1` after `v1.1.0`, because Apple takes no new TestFlight build for a version that is already live. Before the first release, it is the version in `pubspec.yaml`.
- **A tag `v<major>.<minor>.<patch>` on `main` releases the app.** See [Release the app](#release-the-app).

One counter, the run number, numbers every build on every branch and tag, so each build is higher than all before it.

- **Android** ([`mobile-android.yml`](../.github/workflows/mobile-android.yml)): keeps the speech package's native build, its NDK and Gradle's build cache between runs, keyed by `pubspec.lock` and the Flutter version. It signs with the upload key in these repository secrets:
  `RELAY_ANDROID_KEYSTORE_BASE64`, `RELAY_ANDROID_KEYSTORE_PASSWORD`, `RELAY_ANDROID_KEY_ALIAS`, `RELAY_ANDROID_KEY_PASSWORD`. When they are missing, a branch or beta file is signed with a debug key, and a release stops with the names of the missing secrets. A file signed with a debug key can never update to a signed one.
- **iPhone** ([`mobile-ios.yml`](../.github/workflows/mobile-ios.yml)): on a Mac, `flutter build ios --config-only` sets the version and build number, and fastlane compiles the app once and signs it with one saved Apple Distribution certificate. Its `build` lane stops after the check of the speech parts; its `beta` lane also sends the build to TestFlight. Each build gets the app's App Store profiles from Apple, and never makes a certificate. It needs a paid Apple Developer Program membership, the app's record in App Store Connect (bundle id `com.matthewwong525.relayApp`; the watch app and its watch face button use `com.matthewwong525.relayApp.watchkitapp` and `com.matthewwong525.relayApp.watchkitapp.widget`, which the build registers with Apple when they are missing), and these secrets:
  `ASC_ISSUER_ID`, `ASC_KEY_ID`, `ASC_KEY_P8` (the `.p8` file in base64), `APPLE_TEAM_ID`, and `IOS_DIST_CERT_P12` and `IOS_DIST_CERT_PASSWORD`, which [`scripts/ios-signing-cert.mjs`](../scripts/ios-signing-cert.mjs) makes. Until they are set, the iPhone job ends with a notice that names the missing secrets and builds nothing; a release or a release check stops with those names instead.

- **iPhone device check** ([`mobile-device-check.yml`](../.github/workflows/mobile-device-check.yml)): only on request, never on a push. It builds the app for the iPhone simulator on a Mac, opens it, runs a fixed tour of Home and the notes list, a new note and Settings, and lets Claude try the change, while a second Mac runs the Swift tests. You get a report, screenshots, a video and Claude's step log. A newer check of the branch stops the older one. Start it with `/verify`, or put the label *check on iPhone* on a pull request. It needs no Apple key; Claude needs the secret `CLAUDE_CODE_OAUTH_TOKEN` (a Claude subscription token) or `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`. See [the device check](../wiki/development/device-check.md).

Never commit a key, a keystore, a `.p8` file or a signing profile.

### Release the app

1. **Before the first release, fill in the App Store page.** In App Store Connect, add the description, screenshots, support and privacy addresses, age rating and privacy answers, and put the final privacy policy at its address. Apple needs them for its first review. Add Apple Watch screenshots too: the build holds a watch app, and App Store Connect refuses review without them. A release changes only the *What's New* text.
2. **Check the steps with no release.** On GitHub, open *Actions*, then *Mobile CI*, then *Run workflow*, and tick `release_check`. Or run `gh workflow run mobile-ci.yml --ref main -f release_check=true`. Start it from `main` only: from a branch, it stops at once, because its build goes to TestFlight. The run does every step of a release with the next beta's version, and its iPhone build goes to TestFlight with the betas. But it sends nothing to App Store review, makes a draft GitHub release and deletes it, and marks no pull request.
3. **Tag a commit on `main`**, such as `v1.1.0`. On GitHub, open *Releases*, then *Draft a new release*, type the new tag, pick `main`, and publish: the run then adds the `.apk` and the notes to that release, and if the run fails, delete the release. Or run `git tag v1.1.0 origin/main && git push origin v1.1.0`. The tag sets the version. A tag that is not on `main`, is not in this form, or is not higher than the last release stops the run at once, with the reason.

The tag's run then:

1. Runs the full test: the analyzer and the Flutter tests, both signed builds with the check of the speech parts, and the fixed tour of [the device check](../wiki/development/device-check.md) on an iPhone simulator, each step required, with no Claude. One failure stops the release before anything goes to a store.
2. Writes the release notes with `app-release.mjs notes`: each pull request since the last `v*` tag that changed `mobile/`, grouped by its label (`enhancement` is *New*, `bug` is *Fixes*, the rest *Changes*). Changes to the server and the website are not listed.
3. Sends the iPhone build to App Store review with the notes as *What's New* (fastlane's `submit` lane). The first version in the App Store gets no *What's New*, because Apple takes none for it. Apple puts it live as soon as it approves.
4. Publishes the GitHub release *Relay Everything <version>* with the signed `.apk`, marked latest, so the website's *Android app* link opens it.
5. Marks each listed pull request with the label *released* and one comment, with `app-release.mjs mark`.

The job `release` can run again for the same tag: it does not send a version that is already in review or live, it edits the GitHub release, and it does not comment twice. To run a failed release again, use *Re-run failed jobs*, or re-run only the job `release`, never *Re-run all jobs*: that builds the iPhone app again with the same build number, and Apple refuses it.

**Apple takes one version in review at a time.** A new tag while the last version is still in review stops at `submit` with Apple's state. Run the job `release` again when that review ends.

## Where the code is

- `lib/data/`: the settings, the local store (SQLite through drift) that keeps every note, the sender that gives no API client while sending is off, the API client, and the outbox that sends waiting uploads in order.
- `lib/sync/`: health, location, photos and the hourly background sync, which run only while sending is on. On an iPhone, `native.dart` and `wake.dart` talk to `ios/Runner/RelayBackground.swift`, which keeps places and wakes the closed app for Health and places.
- `lib/voice/`: voice notes: the speech model's download, the recorder, and the words, written by whisper.cpp on the phone.
- `lib/shortcuts/`: the Apple Shortcuts bridge. `ios/Runner/RelayIntents.swift` has the actions; they talk to the app's Dart code on the `relay/shortcuts` channel, in the open app or in a hidden engine that runs `shortcutsMain`. A note the app could not take in time waits in `shortcuts-inbox/`, and the app keeps it on its next open.
- `lib/screens/`: Home with its folders, search, the Settings gear and *New note*; the notes list with its day groups; the Location, Health, Calendar and Photos pages; the recording screen, the note page, new note, settings with its Account row, the Account page and sign-in.
- `test/`: unit tests for the store, outbox, API, sync, voice notes and Shortcuts, widget tests for each screen, and a test that no screen or job calls the network while sending is off.
