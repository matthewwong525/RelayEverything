# Relay Everything app

Relay Everything keeps your thoughts. Type a note on your phone and it stays on your phone: you need no account, no key and no signal, and the app sends nothing. When you want to do more with your notes, sign in: each note then goes to your own Relay Everything server, which can pass it to your own database and your AI. Once you sign in, the app can also send your steps, heart rate, sleep, workouts and a history of the places you went. Your AI reads your health and places; this app only sends them.

This is the phone app, for iPhone and Android. It is written in Flutter. The same app, built for the browser, is the Relay Everything page at the top address of each server, such as `https://app.relayeverything.com/`: [The web build](#the-web-build).

## What it does

- **Home.** The app opens on *Home*, a screen of folders like Apple Notes' *Folders*. The first card holds one *Notes* row with the count of all notes. *On this phone* holds *Location*, *Health*, *Calendar* and *Photos*, each with its status, such as *Off* or *Save battery · Sent 14:05*. *Other connections* holds *Connect your AI* (*Let your AI read your notes*) and *Email notes* (*Email a note to your own address*). Home has no setup: your account is in Settings. Tap a header to fold it; the phone remembers. *Search* and *New note* stay at the bottom. Under the *Home* title, a line shows only while notes wait or can not go.
- **Notes.** One list holds every note, newest first, under *Notes*, with *‹ Home* at the top left, in groups: *Today*, *Yesterday*, *Previous 7 days*, *Previous 30 days*, then by month. Pinned notes show first, in a *Pinned* card that a tap folds; the phone remembers. A swipe right on a row pins or unpins it, and a swipe left deletes it. The filters *All*, *Voice* and *Photos and files* above it each show their count; a link alone does not put a note in *Photos and files*. Each row shows the first line in bold, the time, a microphone and the length for a voice note, the rest of the words, *Waiting to send* only on a note that waits, and a small picture if the note has one. No row has a tag. Under the title, a line gives the count, and a state only when you can act on it, such as *5 notes · 2 waiting* or *Key refused*: words, not a button. Offline with nothing waiting, it shows only the count. Scrolled, the top is one row: the small title with the count, or the state in its place. Waiting notes send by themselves, also when the phone gets a connection again; a pull down syncs now, and no button does. Tap a row to open the whole note: the date alone at the top, the voice player under it for a voice note (play and pause, sound waves to tap or drag, *0:12 / 0:42*), then the words, pictures and files. Tap the words to change them, as in Apple Notes. While you type, *Undo* and *Redo* show at the top. The ⋯ menu has *Pin Note*, *Find in Note* (on the phone only), *Copy* and *Delete*.
- **Search.** Type in *Search* at the bottom, and the list shows only the notes with those words in their words or their file names. On Home it finds every note; in the notes list, only the notes of the filter you picked. It searches this phone only, with no signal. *Cancel* shows all the notes again.
- **Settings and your page.** The round gear button at the top right of Home opens *Settings*. Your Relay Everything page is at `<your-relay-server>/` in a browser, where you sign in: this same app, built for the browser. A tap on *Delete my account*, *Add or change on your Relay Everything page*, or an AI app or a computer to disconnect, opens it.
- **New note.** Tap the round green *New note* button at the bottom, type, and tap *Save*. The first line shows in bold. This works with no signal. A note keeps no place: with *Location history* on, the phone adds one point to the location history at the note's time, with its street name, and the note does not wait for it. A shared note, or one that waited such as on the watch, adds no point.
- **Voice notes.** Hold *New note*: an amber circle fills round it in half a second, the phone buzzes, and a full screen opens with a clock and amber bars that follow your voice. Until the first voice note, a *Hold to talk* tip shows above the button. Let go: it keeps listening. Talk, then tap anywhere on the screen, or use the back gesture, to save, or tap the *X* at the top right to keep nothing. The bars use the sound level only to draw the screen. The phone writes the words itself with whisper.cpp, with no signal and no server (on an iPhone, on its processor, so the words come also when the app is not on the screen), and the recording stays on the phone, out of iCloud and Google backups. The first time, the app asks before it gets the 57 MB speech model from whisper.cpp's page on Hugging Face; *Remove* in *Settings* deletes it. A tap shorter than half a second opens *New note* and records nothing. A recording with no words keeps nothing. When the words do not come, the note keeps its recording, its page gives the reason on a *Why* line, and the app tries again when you come back to it, or on *Try again*. With sending on, only the words go to your server, as a typed note.
- **Photos and files.** In *New note*, tap *Add photo or file* to add up to 10 photos or files, each up to 25 MB. They stay on the phone and show on the note's row and its page; tap one to open it. With sending on, each file goes after its note.
- **Apple Shortcuts** (iPhone, iOS 16 and later). Three actions: *Add a note*, *Get notes* and *Start a voice note*. *Start a voice note* opens the same full-screen recording: tap anywhere to save, or the *X* to keep nothing. A note from a shortcut is kept and sent as a typed note is. Siri asks *What's the note?* when *Add a note* has nothing, and also knows the app by a short name: *take a note in Relay*. *Get notes* reads only this phone.
- **Apple Watch** (watchOS 10 and later). The Relay Everything watch app comes with the iPhone app. It records as soon as you open it, from the app list, a watch face button or the *Start a voice note* shortcut, such as on the Action button. Talk, then tap anywhere to save. Leaving the app, with the Digital Crown, saves too; lowering your wrist for a moment does not. The *X* keeps nothing, and under 1 second keeps nothing. After a save, the watch shows *Sending to your iPhone*, then *On your iPhone* when the iPhone has the note, and how many other notes wait; tap for a new note. When the iPhone is near, the watch also starts the iPhone app, so the note gets there in seconds. The iPhone writes the words, also while it is locked, and keeps the note in *Notes*, under the *Voice* filter, as a voice note made on the phone. A watch note with no words stays as *Voice note* with *No words heard* and its recording to play, and is not sent until you write words in it. A watch note keeps no place, and the watch sends nothing to a server.
- **Share from any app.** Share a web page, text, photos or a file into Relay Everything, add words, and tap *Save*. On iPhone and Android, the share card has the brand's paper color, the Inter font and a green *Save*, in light and dark.
- **Settings.** Open it with the gear on Home. The account card comes first, as the Apple Account row on an iPhone. Then two groups: *Appearance* (*System*, *Light* or *Dark*) and *Voice notes* (the speech model). The last line gives the app's version and build. There is no *Advanced* row: the developer options are at the bottom of the Account page.
- **Sign in.** Signed out, the account card says *Sign in to Relay Everything* and *Your notes stay on this phone until you sign in.* A tap opens *Sign in*: one button, *Sign in*, and one quiet link, *Don't want an account? Use your own webhook*. There is no QR code, pasted key or other server: the phone signs in only to Relay Everything, and a phone already set to another server keeps working. Signing in starts sending in the same step, to one place, never both. The webhook setup has four numbered steps: *Your webhook's address* (it must start with `https://`), *Copy this secret to your receiver* (*Copy*), *Check that it works* (*Send a test*), and *Save*. It needs no account: [`lib/data/webhook.dart`](lib/data/webhook.dart) signs each message in the server's webhook format. If you have notes from before, the app asks if they go too: *Send them*, or *Only new notes*. Signed in, the card shows your email and *Signed in*; with your own webhook, *My own webhook* and the webhook's host.
- **The Account page.** A tap on the account card, once you sign in, opens it: your email and *Signed in*, with *Sign out* beside them; *Phones*, each of your phones by name, this one marked *This phone*, each other with *Sign out*, for a lost or sold phone; *Advanced*, with *Webhooks* (*Send a copy to your own server*); and *Delete my account*, last, in red, which opens your Relay Everything page. *Sign out* asks first, then removes this phone's key and stops sending. Your notes from this phone stay; notes from your other devices leave this phone. With your own webhook and no account, the page shows its address, *Send a test*, *Sign in to Relay Everything* and *Stop sending*, which keeps the webhook's address and secret on the phone. *Sign in to Relay Everything* opens the sign-in with no webhook link, and says that everything then goes to Relay Everything; once signed in, notes, health, places, calendar and photos all go to Relay Everything, the app asks whether to send the notes that went only to the webhook, and a message says to add the webhook on your Relay Everything page to keep getting everything there. The phone keeps the webhook's address and secret for a later *Sign out*.
- **Webhooks.** *Webhooks*, under *Advanced* on the Account page, says it is for developers and not needed, lists your webhooks by name, and has *Add or change on your Relay Everything page*. It holds no choice of where data goes: signed in, the phone sends everything to Relay Everything, never to a webhook itself, and Relay Everything sends each webhook your notes and what else you tick for it. To keep data off Relay Everything, use the app with no account and your own webhook. A phone that had *Only to my own webhook* on before it went away sends its health, places, calendar and photos, with what waited, to Relay Everything after the update, and Home says so once: *Everything now goes through Relay Everything*, with *OK* and *Open Webhooks*.
- **Health** (only with sending on). The app reads steps, heart rate, sleep and workouts from Apple Health or Health Connect from the moment you turn it on, and sends them about every hour, also while the app is closed. The *Health* folder shows when they last went. Signed out, it says Health works once you sign in, and *Sign in* opens the same sign-in as Settings.
- **Location history** (only with sending on), in the *Location* folder. On Android, the app keeps a point each 100 m you move. On an iPhone, the phone keeps points while the app is closed: *Save battery* at each move of about 500 m and each arrival, or *Every 100 m*.
- **Photos** (only with sending on), in the *Photos* folder. The app sends a copy of each new photo you take, at most 1,600 px on its long side, with when and where you took it, so your AI can see it. Photos wait for Wi-Fi unless *Send photos on mobile data* is on, and never hold back a note. They go where your notes go: your Relay Everything server, or, with no account, your own webhook as `photo.created` messages. To add a photo to one note, use *Add photo or file*.
- **Connect your AI and Email notes.** Under *Other connections* on Home. *Connect your AI* has *Claude Code and Codex*, your computers with the plugin and when each last saved a chat, then *Saved chats* and *Get the plugin*; and *Claude, ChatGPT and other AI apps*, the connector address `https://mcp.relayeverything.com` with *Copy*, and each connected app with the day it connected. To disconnect one, it opens your Relay Everything page. *Email notes* shows two addresses, each with *Copy*: the shared `notes@` address, which takes mail only from the email you sign in with, and your private address, which takes mail from anyone. *New address* is on the web.
- **Your server, your key.** The app talks only to the server you connect it to, with a key for this phone alone. The key adds data, reads and changes your notes and their files, and reads your account's email, the names of your phones, computers, AI apps and webhooks, and your two notes addresses. It can sign out another of your phones. It reads no health, places, calendar events, chats or photos. You can sign out one phone and keep the others.

Each permission is asked for only when you turn on its setting. Signed out, the app keeps no health or location history and makes no network call, except to get the speech model after you tap *Get it*.

## Get the app

- **Android:** download the `.apk` file from [the newest release](https://github.com/matthewwong525/RelayEverything/releases/latest) and open it on your phone. Android asks once to allow installs from your browser. Test builds from `main` are on [Releases](https://github.com/matthewwong525/RelayEverything/releases), marked *Pre-release*.
- **iPhone:** install TestFlight from the App Store, then open the [public beta](https://testflight.apple.com/join/hSfKtWB7) on the iPhone. You need no invite. After the first App Store release, the App Store has it too.

## Connect it to a server

Each phone sends with its own key, which it gets only by signing in. In the app, open Settings, tap *Sign in to Relay Everything*, then *Sign in*. Your Relay Everything page makes no phone key and no QR code.

The app opens Relay Everything's sign-in page inside the app, the person taps *Connect*, and the app trades the page's one-time code, with a secret only it holds, for its own key at `sign-in/phone/key`. The key holds the server's address. The app signs in only to `app.relayeverything.com`; a phone already set to another server keeps working there. The app checks the key with the server's `key` route, which gives only the device name, never your data. The `connections` route gives only your account's email, the names of your phones, computers, AI apps and webhooks, and your two notes addresses. To try a preview, open its address in a browser.

*Sign out*, beside your email on the Account page, removes the key and turns sending off. Your notes from this phone stay on the phone.

The key is kept in the iPhone Keychain or the Android Keystore, never in the app's own files.

## Build it yourself

1. Install Flutter (the version is in `pubspec.yaml`): https://docs.flutter.dev/get-started/install
2. Get the packages: `flutter pub get`
3. Check and test: `flutter analyze` and `flutter test`
4. Run it on a phone: `flutter run`

### The web build

The Relay Everything page at a server's `/` is this app, built for the browser from its own entry, [`lib/main_web.dart`](lib/main_web.dart). It signs in against the server it is served from, with that server's sign-in, and keeps no notes, key or health data in the browser. A wide window shows three panes like Notes on a Mac; under 700 px it shows the phone layout. What the page does is on the website: [On a computer](https://relayeverything.com/app/on-a-computer/).

- **Try it:** `flutter run -d chrome -t lib/main_web.dart`.
- **Build it:** `node scripts/build-relay-web.mjs` runs `flutter build web -t lib/main_web.dart --base-href /apps/relay/ --no-web-resources-cdn --release`, then copies `mobile/build/web/` to `app/public/apps/relay/`. The server opens the page at `/`, and loads its files, API and sign-in from `/apps/relay/`. `scripts/publish-relay-server.mjs` and the release zip run it; the company app's build does not.
- **No Google fonts:** the engine gets its fonts for emoji and other scripts from `fonts.gstatic.com` unless told otherwise. [`web/flutter_bootstrap.js`](web/flutter_bootstrap.js) points it at the page's own `fallback-fonts/`, and the build script reads the engine's font list from the built `main.dart.js` and fetches each file there, once, into `mobile/build/fallback-fonts/`. Under `flutter run`, emoji show as boxes, because that folder is missing.

### Builds on GitHub

Each push that changes `mobile/` runs [`.github/workflows/mobile-ci.yml`](../.github/workflows/mobile-ci.yml). Its first job, `plan`, runs [`scripts/app-release.mjs`](../scripts/app-release.mjs) `version` to get the run's version and build number: you never edit the version in `pubspec.yaml`. Then it analyzes and tests, and builds both apps:

- **A branch or pull request builds and sends nothing.** Both builds start at the same time as the tests, so the run ends in about 6 minutes; a failed test still makes the run fail. The `.apk` is kept with the run, for 64-bit ARM phones only, and the iPhone app is signed and checked, so a broken build shows before it lands.
- **A push to `main` sends a beta.** Its builds start only after the tests pass, and its `.apk` holds every kind of Android phone. TestFlight gets the iPhone build, then the [public beta](#public-beta) gets it too, and a *Pre-release* tagged `mobile-v<version>+<build>` gets the `.apk`. It is never the latest release. Only the newest change sends: when a newer commit on `main` changes `mobile/`, the older run sends nothing, as the newer run sends its own build. A beta's version is the newest release's with its last number one higher, such as `1.1.1` after `v1.1.0`, because Apple takes no new TestFlight build for a version that is already live. Before the first release, it is the version in `pubspec.yaml`.
- **A tag `v<major>.<minor>.<patch>` on `main` releases the app.** See [Release the app](#release-the-app).

One counter, the run number, numbers every build on every branch and tag, so each build is higher than all before it.

- **Android** ([`mobile-android.yml`](../.github/workflows/mobile-android.yml)): keeps the speech package's native build, its NDK and Gradle's build cache between runs, keyed by `pubspec.lock` and the Flutter version. It signs with the upload key in these repository secrets:
  `RELAY_ANDROID_KEYSTORE_BASE64`, `RELAY_ANDROID_KEYSTORE_PASSWORD`, `RELAY_ANDROID_KEY_ALIAS`, `RELAY_ANDROID_KEY_PASSWORD`. When they are missing, a branch or beta file is signed with a debug key, and a release stops with the names of the missing secrets. A file signed with a debug key can never update to a signed one.
- **iPhone** ([`mobile-ios.yml`](../.github/workflows/mobile-ios.yml)): on a Mac, `flutter build ios --config-only` sets the version and build number, and fastlane compiles the app once and signs it with one saved Apple Distribution certificate. Its `build` lane stops after the check of the speech parts; its `beta` lane also sends the build to TestFlight. Each build gets the app's App Store profiles from Apple, and never makes a certificate. It needs a paid Apple Developer Program membership, the app's record in App Store Connect (bundle id `com.matthewwong525.relayApp`; the watch app and its watch face button use `com.matthewwong525.relayApp.watchkitapp` and `com.matthewwong525.relayApp.watchkitapp.widget`, which the build registers with Apple when they are missing), and these secrets:
  `ASC_ISSUER_ID`, `ASC_KEY_ID`, `ASC_KEY_P8` (the `.p8` file in base64), `APPLE_TEAM_ID`, and `IOS_DIST_CERT_P12` and `IOS_DIST_CERT_PASSWORD`, which [`scripts/ios-signing-cert.mjs`](../scripts/ios-signing-cert.mjs) makes. Until they are set, the iPhone job ends with a notice that names the missing secrets and builds nothing; a release or a release check stops with those names instead.

- **iPhone device check** ([`mobile-device-check.yml`](../.github/workflows/mobile-device-check.yml)): only on request, never on a push. It builds the app for the iPhone simulator on a Mac, opens it, runs a fixed tour of Home and the notes list, a new note and Settings, and lets Claude try the change, while a second Mac runs the Swift tests. You get a report, screenshots, a video and Claude's step log. A newer check of the branch stops the older one. Start it with `/verify`, or put the label *check on iPhone* on a pull request. It needs no Apple key; Claude needs the secret `CLAUDE_CODE_OAUTH_TOKEN` (a Claude subscription token) or `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`. See [the device check](../wiki/development/device-check.md).

Never commit a key, a keystore, a `.p8` file or a signing profile.

### Release the app

1. **Check the App Store page.** Its words, screenshots, categories and age rating are files in [`ios/fastlane/`](#the-app-store-page), and the release sends them before the build. Before a tag that makes the app send a new kind of data, make the privacy answers in App Store Connect the same as `app_privacy_details.json`.
2. **Check the steps with no release.** On GitHub, open *Actions*, then *Mobile CI*, then *Run workflow*, and tick `release_check`. Or run `gh workflow run mobile-ci.yml --ref main -f release_check=true`. Start it from `main` only: from a branch, it stops at once, because its build goes to TestFlight. The run does every step of a release with the next beta's version, and its iPhone build goes to TestFlight with the betas. But it sends nothing to App Store review, makes a draft GitHub release and deletes it, and marks no pull request.
3. **Tag a commit on `main`**, such as `v1.1.0`. On GitHub, open *Releases*, then *Draft a new release*, type the new tag, pick `main`, and publish: the run then adds the `.apk` and the notes to that release, and if the run fails, delete the release. Or run `git tag v1.1.0 origin/main && git push origin v1.1.0`. The tag sets the version. A tag that is not on `main`, is not in this form, or is not higher than the last release stops the run at once, with the reason.

The tag's run then:

1. Runs the full test: the analyzer and the Flutter tests, both signed builds with the check of the speech parts, and the fixed tour of [the device check](../wiki/development/device-check.md) on an iPhone simulator, each step required, with no Claude. One failure stops the release before anything goes to a store.
2. Writes the release notes with `app-release.mjs notes`: each pull request since the last `v*` tag that changed `mobile/`, grouped by its label (`enhancement` is *New*, `bug` is *Fixes*, the rest *Changes*). Changes to the server and the website are not listed.
3. Sends the iPhone build to App Store review with the notes as *What's New* (fastlane's `submit` lane). The first version in the App Store gets no *What's New*, because Apple takes none for it. Apple puts it live as soon as it approves.
4. Publishes the GitHub release *Relay Everything <version>* with the signed `.apk`, marked latest, so the website's *Android app* link opens it.
5. Marks each listed pull request with the label *released* and one comment, with `app-release.mjs mark`.

The job `release` can run again for the same tag: it does not send a version that is already in review or live, it edits the GitHub release, and it does not comment twice. To run a failed release again, use *Re-run failed jobs*, or re-run only the job `release`, never *Re-run all jobs*: that builds the iPhone app again with the same build number, and Apple refuses it.

**Apple takes one version in review at a time.** A new tag while the last version is still in review stops at `submit` with Apple's state. Run the job `release` again when that review ends.

### Public beta

Anyone can install the iPhone app from the public link, https://testflight.apple.com/join/hSfKtWB7, with no invite. The link opens the external TestFlight group *Public beta*.

After each beta from `main` goes to TestFlight, the job `public-beta` in [`mobile-ci.yml`](../.github/workflows/mobile-ci.yml) starts the workflow *Mobile public beta*, [`mobile-public-beta.yml`](../.github/workflows/mobile-public-beta.yml), with the build's version, build number and commit. That workflow waits for Apple in its own queue, one build at a time, so the next run of *Mobile CI* on `main` does not wait behind it. It runs fastlane's `public_beta` lane on Ubuntu, for at most 90 minutes. The lane:

1. Waits until Apple has processed the build, and then processed it for TestFlight, for at most 75 minutes in total.
2. Writes the change's title, without its `(#n)`, as the build's *What to Test*, in the app's primary language.
3. Adds the build to *Public beta*.
4. Sends the build to Apple's beta review. Testers with the link get it when Apple approves it. The first build of a new version can wait a day or more; later builds usually pass quickly.

Apple takes one build in beta review at a time. If an earlier build still waits, Apple refuses the new one: the job notes it and passes, and the next change to `mobile/` sends again. A build that Apple can not take for beta review, such as one with missing export compliance, fails the job with Apple's state. You can run *Mobile public beta* again: it does not add or send a build twice. Just after a build is processed, Apple can answer *no resource of type 'builds'* at the beta review step, also when the build is already in *Public beta*. Wait about 20 minutes, then run the job again. A branch, a release and a release check send nothing to the public beta.

**Set by hand in App Store Connect.** The lane only adds builds. Change these in [App Store Connect](https://appstoreconnect.apple.com/), under the app's *TestFlight* tab:

- **The group *Public beta*,** with its public link and feedback on. Keep its name: the lane finds it by `PUBLIC_BETA_GROUP` in the [`Fastfile`](ios/fastlane/Fastfile), and fails with no group of that name. If you make it again, turn on its public link and put the new link on the website and here.
- **The beta description and the privacy link,** under *Test Information*. The privacy link is the website's [Privacy page](https://relayeverything.com/privacy/).
- **The beta review contact,** under *Test Information*: the person Apple calls about a review. Their email and phone stay in App Store Connect, never in this repository.

### The App Store page

The App Store page lives in [`ios/fastlane/`](ios/fastlane/), next to the iPhone app. To change the page, change a file and publish it: the next release sends it. You make no change in App Store Connect.

- **The words:** `metadata/en-CA/` holds the name, subtitle, description, keywords, promotional text, support link, website link and privacy link. `metadata/` holds the copyright line, the two categories, and the note for Apple's reviewer in `review_information/notes.txt`.
- **The age rating:** `rating.json`, the answers to Apple's age rating questions.
- **The screenshots:** `screenshots/en-CA/`, plain screens with no added words, for a 6.9-inch iPhone, a 13-inch iPad and an Apple Watch. Apple takes 1 to 10 for each device.
- **The privacy answers:** `app_privacy_details.json`, in fastlane's format.

[`scripts/app-store-listing.test.mjs`](../scripts/app-store-listing.test.mjs) checks Apple's limits, the links, the categories, the age rating questions and the size of each screenshot. It refuses an email address or a phone number, because the repository is public.

**What a release sends.** Before it submits the build, the release runs fastlane's `listing` lane. It sends the words, screenshots, categories and age rating from the tagged commit, and replaces all the screenshots. A version that is already in review keeps its page. A release check runs `listing verify_only:true`: it checks the screenshots with fastlane's own check, reads App Store Connect, and sends nothing. Apple checks the words, such as a name that another app has, only at the release.

**New screenshots.** Take them again when the app looks different:

1. On GitHub, open *Actions*, then *Mobile store screenshots*, then *Run workflow*. Or put the label *store screenshots* on a pull request. The run makes its own sample notes on a new install, so the pictures show no person's data.
2. Download the file `store-screenshots` from the run. Put its `en-CA/` pictures in `ios/fastlane/screenshots/en-CA/`, in place of the old ones.
3. Look at each picture, then publish the change.

**Change the privacy answers.** Apple has no way for a script to set them. When a change makes the app send a new kind of data, change `app_privacy_details.json` in the same change. Then, before the next release tag, open the app's *App Privacy* page in [App Store Connect](https://appstoreconnect.apple.com/), make the answers the same as the file, and publish them.

**Set once by hand in App Store Connect:** the price, free, in all countries; and the reviewer contact, under *App Review Information*. The contact's email and phone stay in App Store Connect, never in this repository.

## The Mac app

The Mac app is this app, built for macOS from its own entry, [`lib/main_mac.dart`](lib/main_mac.dart). It keeps your notes on the Mac, so they work with no network, and sends them when you sign in from Settings, as a phone does. Its window has the layout of [the web build](#the-web-build): three parts, two, or one as it gets narrower. It has the Mac's menu bar, with *New Note* (⌘N), *Find* (⌘F), *Bold* (⌘B), *Italic* (⌘I) and *Settings…* (⌘,), and it takes files that you drop from Finder on a note.

- **Try it on a Mac:** `flutter run -d macos -t lib/main_mac.dart`. The app needs macOS 12 or newer.
- **Sync:** the app gets new notes every 5 minutes, when the network comes back, and when its window gets focus again. It does not update itself: you download a new version from the website.
- **What it leaves out:** location, health, calendar, photos, the microphone and the speech model. A voice note from a phone shows its words, and a sound file on a note opens in the Mac's own player. [`test/mac_imports_test.dart`](test/mac_imports_test.dart) checks every file the Mac entry reaches, except `lib/controller.dart`, and fails on a phone-only source. [`test/controller_test.dart`](test/controller_test.dart) checks that the controller works with no phone parts.
- **The key** goes in the Mac's login keychain, with no shared group, so the app needs no provisioning profile. The vault reads each of its names by itself, never all at once: the login keychain refuses a read of all items with their values (`errSecParam`, -50), and that error stops the app before its first screen. The device shows as *Mac* on your Relay Everything page.
- **The app** is `com.matthewwong525.relayApp.mac`, named *Relay Everything*, with App Sandbox, network client and the files you pick or drop ([`macos/Runner/Release.entitlements`](macos/Runner/Release.entitlements)). The debug build also has Flutter's two keys for `flutter run`.

**How it is built and signed.** [`mobile-macos.yml`](../.github/workflows/mobile-macos.yml) runs from `mobile-ci.yml`, beside the iPhone build:

- **A branch or pull request** builds the app, signs every part ad hoc with no hardened runtime, and keeps it with the run as `Relay-Everything-Mac.dmg`, the same install window as a release's, so you can check the window before it goes out. It sends nothing. To open its app, run `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/Relay Everything.app"`, or click *Open Anyway* in System Settings → Privacy & Security: since macOS 15, right-click *Open* no longer does it.
- **The install window:** [`macos/dmg/make-dmg.sh`](macos/dmg/make-dmg.sh) makes every disk image with [dmgbuild](https://github.com/dmgbuild/dmgbuild), pinned in the workflow. dmgbuild writes the window's layout from [`macos/dmg/settings.py`](macos/dmg/settings.py) with no Finder, so it runs on a build machine with no person. The background, `macos/dmg/background.png` and its `@2x`, comes from `node brand/make-icons.mjs`: [the brand](../wiki/brand.md#the-logo).
- **`main` and a release** sign the app with the hardened runtime and a Developer ID Application certificate, make `Relay-Everything-Mac.dmg` and sign it, then send it to Apple's check (notarize) with the App Store Connect key and staple Apple's ticket. `node scripts/app-release.mjs mac-check --file Relay-Everything-Mac.dmg` refuses a file that is not signed, checked and stapled. A beta adds the file to its pre-release, and a `v` tag to its release.
- **The certificate:** Apple lets only the Account Holder make a Developer ID Application certificate, so `node scripts/ios-signing-cert.mjs --mac` with the App Store Connect key gets `result=refused` and names the page where the Account Holder makes it by hand. Export it with its key as a `.p12`, and save it as `MACOS_DEVID_CERT_P12` and `MACOS_DEVID_CERT_PASSWORD` in `.env` and the GitHub secrets. With them missing, `main` builds no file, with a notice, and a release stops with their names.

## Where the code is

- `lib/data/`: the settings, the local store (SQLite through drift) that keeps every note, the sender that gives no API client while sending is off, the API client, and the outbox that sends waiting uploads in order.
- `lib/sync/`: health, location, photos and the hourly background sync, which run only while sending is on. On an iPhone, `native.dart` and `wake.dart` talk to `ios/Runner/RelayBackground.swift`, which keeps places and wakes the closed app for Health and places.
- `lib/voice/`: voice notes: the speech model's download, the recorder, and the words, written by whisper.cpp on the phone.
- `lib/editor/`: the note editor's own parts. `paste.dart` keeps the headings, lists, bold and links of pasted words, from web pages, Notion, Google Docs and Markdown from ChatGPT. The Mac app and the web page use the same editor, so they need no paste code of their own.
- `lib/shortcuts/`: the Apple Shortcuts bridge. `ios/Runner/RelayIntents.swift` has the actions; they talk to the app's Dart code on the `relay/shortcuts` channel, in the open app or in a hidden engine that runs `shortcutsMain`. A note the app could not take in time waits in `shortcuts-inbox/`, and the app keeps it on its next open.
- `lib/screens/`: Home with its folders, search, the Settings gear and *New note*; the notes list with its day groups; the Location, Health, Calendar and Photos pages; the recording screen, the note page, new note, settings with its account card, the Account page, sign-in, Connect your AI and Email notes. The note page's tool bar and find bar float over the page's bottom edge, and the editor scrolls the cursor only to that edge, under the bar. So on each move of the cursor the page scrolls it, after the frame, to above the bar (`barRoom`). Do not use the editor's `scrollBottomInset` for this: flutter_quill 11.6 adds it over the cursor too, so a cursor that moves up goes under the page's top edge.
- `lib/editor/`: the note's Markdown, paste, keys and tables. `markdown.dart` keeps every style as Markdown in the note's words, so the server, the AI and webhooks read plain text: a highlight as `<mark class="pink">…</mark>`, a link on words as `[words](address)`, and a table as a Markdown table. In the editor a table is one `table` embed whose JSON holds its id, its rows and its `source`, the Markdown it was read from. The note writes `source` back while the cells are unchanged, so a table Claude wrote keeps its alignment marks. `table.dart` draws its cells as fields outside the editor, and writes a cell's words into the note after each pause as one Undo step. `shortcuts.dart` holds the one list of format items and keys that the browser's keys and the Mac's *Format* menu share.
- `test/`: unit tests for the store, outbox, API, sync, voice notes and Shortcuts, widget tests for each screen, and a test that no screen or job calls the network while sending is off.
